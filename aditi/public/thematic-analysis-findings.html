Reflexive thematic analysis

What people believe learning is for when AI enters the room.

This map compares two opposing YouTube comment datasets. Dataset #1 captures hope: AI as access, emotional safety, and support. Dataset #2 captures threat: AI as corporate control, broken schooling, and the loss of thinking through struggle. The center layer shows what is at stake between those reactions.

Stake 1

The hope that education could finally be fair

Hope

AI might reach people excluded by cost, geography, disability, and institutional neglect.

Threat

That promise may become a paid hierarchy where better learning belongs to people who can afford better tools.

Why it matters

The same technology is read as liberation in one dataset and as inequality in the other. The stake is not AI itself, but whether education can finally be fair.

Dataset #1 themes

Theme 1

AI expands access to learning

Commenters imagine AI as a way to reach students blocked by cost, geography, disability, confidence, or systems that were never designed around them.

Theme 2

AI changes the emotional experience of learning

Learning is framed as easier when students can ask questions privately, repeat confusion without shame, and move at their own pace.

Theme 3

AI changes how students think and learn

The hope is that AI can scaffold difficult ideas, but the comments also begin to question what happens when struggle disappears too quickly.

Dataset #2 themes

Theme A

The system was already broken

Commenters argue AI did not create the crisis. It entered a system already shaped by testing, metrics, and control.

Theme B

AI takes away the ability to think

Learning is framed as something built through friction, effort, intention, and the discomfort of not knowing yet.

Theme C

AI serves corporations, not students

The fear is that education becomes a product tier controlled by companies rather than a public good shaped by communities.

Finding

People are not only arguing about AI.

They are defending what they believe education owes them: equal access, human connection, and the ability to think. Dataset #1 shows those beliefs as hope. Dataset #2 shows them as fear and grief.
